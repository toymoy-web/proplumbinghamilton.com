When to call right away, and what can wait

Some plumbing problems cost more every hour they sit. Others are annoying but safe to leave overnight. Knowing the difference saves money on after-hours rates.

  • Call now: water spraying or pouring from a pipe, sewage coming up a floor drain, a leaking water heater tank, a sump pit about to overflow, or any smell of gas.
  • Call now: no water at all on a freezing morning, since a frozen pipe may split as it thaws.
  • Can usually wait: a dripping faucet, a toilet that runs, one slow sink, or a water heater that still makes some hot water.
  • Can usually wait: a planned water heater or fixture replacement, which is where free estimates apply.

If you are not sure, call anyway. A two-minute conversation will tell you whether it needs someone tonight.

What happens when you call

The person who answers asks what you see, where it is, and whether you have been able to shut the water off. If the water is still running, you get help finding the main shutoff first. For a Hamilton basement home, that is usually on the street side where the service line comes through the foundation wall.

Next you get a realistic arrival window based on the time and your part of Butler County. On site, the cause is diagnosed before any repair begins, and you are given the price for the work before it starts. Permitted jobs, such as water heater replacement, sewer work and water line replacement, are handled under a city Master Registration as Hamilton requires, and are inspected against the Ohio Plumbing Code, which is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC).

Local conditions behind these services

A few Hamilton facts explain why some services here get more calls than they would elsewhere. The city runs its own water, gas, electric and wastewater utilities, the only Ohio city that operates all four. Its water comes from deep wells in a glacial sand and gravel aquifer and is lime-treated at the city plants, while homes on private wells outside the city get harder, untreated water.

Owners are responsible for the full sewer lateral to the public main under city code section 931.031, so root-invaded clay lines are a homeowner repair. And older houses in German Village, Dayton Lane and Rossville are the most likely to have galvanized supply pipe and lead service lines, which is why water line replacement and repiping sit near the top of this list.
