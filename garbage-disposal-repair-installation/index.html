Garbage Disposal Repair & Installation in Hamilton, OH

Garbage disposal repair in Hamilton, OH covers units that hum without spinning, trip their reset button, leak under the sink or drain slowly, along with full replacement. Many problems are jams that clear in minutes. Leaks from the unit body usually mean replacement.

Before you reach inside

  • Never put a hand into the disposal, even with it off.
  • Switch it off and unplug it or turn off its breaker.
  • Press the red reset button on the bottom once.
  • Use the hex wrench slot on the bottom to free a jam.

What not to put down a disposal

  • Grease, oil and fat, which harden in the drain line.
  • Fibrous scraps such as celery, corn husks and onion skins.
  • Coffee grounds and eggshells in large amounts, which pack into sludge.
  • Pasta and rice, which swell with water.
  • Anything that is not food.

Kitchen lines in older Hamilton homes often run a long distance across the basement at a gentle slope. Scraps that make it past the disposal can settle there. If the kitchen sink keeps backing up, our drain cleaning page covers the line itself.

Repair or replace?

Jams, tripped resets and loose flanges are repairs. A disposal that leaks from its body has a failed internal seal and needs replacing, since that seal is not serviced separately on most home units.

When replacing, the motor size should match how the kitchen is used. The dishwasher drain knockout must be removed on a new unit if the dishwasher connects to it, which is a common reason a new install leaves the dishwasher unable to drain.

Dishwasher connections

Most dishwashers drain through a hose into the disposal or a sink tailpiece. The Ohio Plumbing Code, based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), requires protection against dirty water siphoning back into the dishwasher, usually by a high loop or an air gap. Many leaks and odors come from this connection rather than the disposal.

If the leak is from the faucet or supply lines instead, see our faucet and sink repair page.

Septic homes and disposals

Homes on septic systems in the townships around Hamilton should be cautious with disposals. Ground food adds solids to the tank, which means more frequent pumping. If you are on septic, check with your septic service before adding a disposal, and use it lightly if you already have one.
