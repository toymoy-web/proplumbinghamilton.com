Shower & Bathtub Installation in Hamilton, OH

Shower and bathtub installation in Hamilton, OH covers the plumbing side of a new tub, a tub-to-shower conversion, or a replacement shower valve behind the wall. It includes the drain, the valve, and the supply connections that the tile or surround will cover.

Signs the valve needs work

  • Shower drips after it is turned off.
  • Water suddenly turns scalding or cold when a toilet flushes.
  • Stains on the ceiling below the bathroom.
  • The handle is stiff or spins without changing temperature.

The shower valve does most of the work

The valve behind the handle mixes hot and cold water. Modern pressure-balancing or thermostatic valves keep the temperature steady when someone flushes a toilet elsewhere. The Ohio Plumbing Code, based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), calls for shower valves that protect against scalding, which is why a very old two-handle valve is usually replaced during a remodel rather than reused.

A dripping shower is often a worn cartridge that can be replaced without opening the wall. A leak behind the wall, from the valve body or its connections, needs access. In many homes that access is through the back of a closet or the wall of the next room.

Older Hamilton bathrooms bring their own surprises

Bathrooms in pre-1920 homes in German Village, Dayton Lane and Rossville often have cast iron tubs, lead or cast iron drain connections under the floor, and galvanized supply lines inside plaster walls. A tub swap there tends to reveal old drain work that needs replacing at the same time.

Cast iron tubs are extremely heavy, so removal is planned. It is usually easier to break an old tub in place than to carry it out whole. If the supply pipes feeding the bathroom are galvanized, doing a repipe of that bathroom while the walls are open saves money later.

Order of work on a remodel

  • Demolition exposes the old valve and drain.
  • Rough-in plumbing is installed and pressure-tested.
  • The city inspects the rough-in on permitted work before walls close.
  • Tile or surround is installed by the finish trade.
  • Trim, handles and fixtures are set and tested.

Check your water heater before adding fixtures

A larger shower head, a body spray system or a big soaking tub can use more hot water than the existing water heater can supply. Before choosing fixtures, check the heater's size and age. Pairing a remodel with a heater upgrade avoids a new bathroom that runs cold halfway through a shower.

What shower and tub work costs depends on

The plumbing cost depends heavily on what is behind the wall. These factors matter most.
