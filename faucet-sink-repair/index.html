Faucet & Sink Repair in Hamilton, OH

Faucet and sink repair in Hamilton, OH fixes drips, leaks under the sink, low flow at one tap, stiff handles and failed shutoff valves, plus installation of new faucets. Most of these jobs are about one worn part.

Check under the sink

  • Wet cabinet floor or swollen particleboard.
  • Drips from the supply line nuts when the faucet runs.
  • A trap that drips at its slip joints.
  • Shutoff valves that will not turn or that leak at the stem.

What usually fails in a faucet

Most modern faucets use a cartridge or ceramic disc to control flow. When the cartridge wears, the faucet drips from the spout or leaks around the handle. Older two-handle faucets use rubber washers and seats that wear the same way.

Low flow at one faucet but not the others usually points to a clogged aerator or cartridge screen. Unscrew the aerator at the tip and rinse it. If mineral or grit is packed inside, that is the problem.

Old shutoff valves are worth replacing

Many older Hamilton homes still have the original multi-turn shutoff valves under sinks and toilets. After decades of sitting open, they often will not close, or they start leaking the moment someone turns them. That becomes a problem at the worst time, in an emergency.

The Ohio Plumbing Code, based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), expects fixtures such as sinks and toilets to have their own supply shutoff valves. Replacing old ones with quarter-turn valves during a faucet repair costs little and means you can shut water off at that fixture without going to the basement. It is a small upgrade that pays off the first time something leaks.

Installing a new faucet

  • Measure the sink holes before buying, since faucets fit one, two, three or four holes.
  • Check that the new faucet fits the counter thickness.
  • Replace old supply lines with new braided ones.
  • Test for leaks under the sink with the water running for several minutes.

If a sink drains slowly after the faucet work, the issue is the drain, not the faucet. See drain cleaning.

Laundry tubs and utility sinks

Basement laundry tubs in older Hamilton homes often take the washing machine discharge as well as their own faucet. Lint collects in the trap and the drain line below it, and the faucet itself sees heavy use. A simple lint catcher on the washer hose saves the drain, and replacing a worn utility faucet costs far less than the water it wastes.

What faucet and sink repair costs depends on

Most of these repairs are quick. These factors change the price.

  • Repair or replace the faucet.
  • Cartridge availability for your brand and model.
  • Shutoff valve replacement if the old valves are seized.
  • Access in tight cabinets or pedestal sinks.
  • Timing of the visit.

Faucet wear is maintenance and not covered. Sudden damage from a burst supply line may be covered, so inspect braided lines every few years.
