Water Line Repair & Replacement in Hamilton, OH

Water line repair and replacement in Hamilton, OH deals with the buried pipe that brings city water from the curb stop into your house. It is a different job from repiping, which replaces the pipes inside the walls. When this line leaks, bursts or turns out to be lead, the property owner is typically responsible for the private side.

We repair leaks on that line and replace it completely when it is lead, galvanized or failing. Call (513) 796-9381 to talk it through.

Is it the service line?

  • The meter moves with every tap in the house turned off.
  • A wet or unusually green strip of lawn between house and street.
  • Pressure dropped at every fixture at once.
  • The pipe entering the basement is soft gray metal.

Meter spinning, soggy lawn or low pressure everywhere?

Most owners never see their service line. What they notice is the result. A bill that climbed while your habits stayed the same, a meter that keeps moving when nothing is running, or a patch of yard that never dries all point to a leak between the curb stop and the house.

A quick test helps. Turn off every faucet and appliance, note the meter reading, and wait 30 minutes without using water. If it moved, something is leaking. Then close the main shutoff inside the house and check again. If the meter still moves, the leak is on the buried service line rather than inside the house.

If the meter stops when the inside main is closed, the leak is in the house plumbing, not the service line. Our leak detection service finds leaks inside walls and floors.

Lead service lines in Hamilton: what the city covers and what you own

Hamilton's water utility, operated by the city since 1884, is replacing lead service lines under the federal Lead and Copper Rule. The city built a service-line inventory, used a prediction model to estimate materials where records are missing, and made the map public. Customers with known or possible lead lines were notified by mail.

Responsibility splits at the curb-stop valve. The city's program covers the public stretch from the main to the curb stop. Past the curb stop, the pipe into your house belongs to you, and so do its repairs and eventual replacement.

The best time to replace the private side is when the city is working on your street, since the trench and the water shutoff can be coordinated. Replacing only half of a lead line can temporarily disturb the remaining lead, so both sides should be done around the same time where possible.

Check your own line before you call

  • Look up your address on the city's public service-line map.
  • Find where the water pipe enters the basement, usually before the meter.
  • Scratch the pipe gently with a key. Lead shows shiny silver and is soft.
  • A magnet will not stick to lead or copper. It will stick to galvanized steel.
  • If it looks like lead, run the cold tap until it turns noticeably colder before drinking, and use cold water for cooking.

Repair or full replacement?

A single leak on a copper or plastic line in otherwise good condition can often be dug out and repaired with a compression or brass coupling. A lead line or an old galvanized line should be replaced, not patched, because the rest of the pipe is the same age and material as the part that failed.

Replacement pipe is usually type K copper or a polyethylene service tubing rated for buried potable water. Both are proven. The choice depends on soil, length and how the line will be installed.

The Ohio Plumbing Code, based on the 2021 IPC, requires water service pipe to be protected from freezing, so the new line has to be buried deep enough to stay below frost, and it ties into a new shutoff inside the house. It is pressure-tested before the trench is closed.

How the curb stop and permits work

The curb stop is the valve at the property line that shuts off water to the house. It is utility equipment, so a replacement is coordinated with the city water department rather than done by forcing an old valve. Water line replacement is typically permitted work in Hamilton, pulled by a contractor with a city Master Registration.

Utility marking through Ohio 811 comes before any digging. On a planned replacement, water is usually off for part of one day. If the old line is leaking badly, a temporary repair can stop the water loss while the permanent job is scheduled.

Homeowners in Fairfield are served by a separate water system, so ownership rules there follow that city's policy. If the pipes inside your house are also galvanized or lead-soldered, our repiping page covers replacing them in the same project.

What determines the price of a water line job

No two service lines are the same length or depth. These factors set the price.

  • Length of the run from the curb stop to where the line enters the house.
  • Installation method. Pulling or boring costs differently than an open trench, and saves landscaping.
  • What is on top. Driveways, walks, trees and retaining walls add work or require trenchless methods.
  • Pipe material and the new shutoff valve inside the house.
  • Permits and city coordination for the curb stop connection.

Homeowners insurance rarely pays to replace an aging service line. Some policies or add-on plans cover service line breaks, and lead line programs may offset part of the cost, so check with your insurer and the city before the work starts.
