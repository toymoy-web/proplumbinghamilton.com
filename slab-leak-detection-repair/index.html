Slab Leak Detection & Repair in Hamilton, OH

Slab leak repair in Hamilton, OH deals with a pipe leaking under a concrete floor. That can be the slab of a ranch house or the basement floor of an older home. It is a separate job from general leak detection because the pipe is buried in or under concrete, which changes how it is found and how it is reached.

The first step is always confirming the leak and its location, so the floor is opened in one small spot or not at all. Call (513) 796-9381.

Signs of a leak under concrete

  • A warm spot on the floor above a hot water line.
  • Damp or darkened concrete with no leak above it.
  • The sound of water running under the floor.
  • A meter that moves with every fixture off.

Slab leaks in a basement city

Slab leaks are less common in Hamilton than in cities where most homes sit on a concrete slab. Here, many houses have full basements with supply lines hung from the joists where they are easy to see. That changes where slab leaks actually happen.

The two most common cases are ranch homes built on a slab, where supply lines run under or through the concrete, and older basements where drain lines run under the floor to the main. A damp patch on a basement floor can be groundwater, a floor drain problem or a buried drain line, and each has a different fix.

If the damp floor tracks with rain rather than water use, the fix is more likely a sump pump or drainage issue than a slab leak.

Repair options for a pipe under concrete

  • Spot repair. Cut a small opening in the concrete at the pinpointed spot, repair the pipe, then patch the floor.
  • Reroute. Abandon the buried line and run a new one through walls or ceilings, avoiding the slab entirely.
  • Line the drain. For a cracked drain under a basement floor, a liner can repair it from inside without breaking concrete, if the pipe has not collapsed.

When a buried supply line has leaked once and is old, rerouting is often the longer-lasting answer, since the rest of the line is the same age. The Ohio Plumbing Code, based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), requires pipe passing through concrete to be protected against corrosion, which older installations often lacked.

How this differs from general leak detection

Our leak detection service covers leaks in walls, ceilings and exposed floors. Slab work needs extra steps: isolating buried lines, listening through concrete, and planning a repair that leaves a sound floor. The two are kept separate so you are not charged for concrete work on a leak that is in a wall.

Slab leak work in Hamilton and nearby

We locate and repair under-slab leaks in ZIP codes 45011, 45013 and 45015. Postwar and later slab ranches in Fairfield are among the homes where supply lines run under concrete.

What slab leak repair costs depends on

Finding the leak and repairing it are priced separately, and the repair choice matters most.

  • Detection time to isolate and pinpoint the line.
  • Repair method: spot repair, reroute or drain lining.
  • Floor covering over the leak, such as tile or hardwood.
  • Concrete patching and finish work.
  • Drain versus supply, since the methods differ.

Some policies cover the cost of accessing a sudden slab leak and the resulting damage, but not the pipe. Coverage varies widely, so check before work begins.
