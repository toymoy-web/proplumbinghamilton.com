Repiping & Whole-Home Pipe Replacement in Hamilton, OH

Repiping in Hamilton, OH means swapping out the supply plumbing behind your walls, from the basement shutoff to every faucet, toilet and appliance. It is the lasting fix for rusty morning water, pinhole leaks that keep coming back, and showers that weaken year after year.

The underground pipe from the street is a separate job, though owners often schedule both together. Estimates for repiping are free. Call (513) 796-9381.

Signs your home needs a repipe

  • Rusty or brown water when a tap first opens.
  • Low pressure upstairs, especially with two taps running.
  • More than one leak on the supply pipes in a year.
  • Visible galvanized steel pipe with corroded joints.

Why galvanized pipe fails in older Hamilton houses

Galvanized steel was a standard supply pipe in homes built through the mid-1900s, which covers much of German Village, Dayton Lane, Rossville and many older blocks around them. The zinc coating wears away over decades and the steel inside rusts, narrowing the pipe from the inside.

That is why pressure fades slowly over the years and why leaks show up at threaded joints. Patching one failed joint rarely helps for long, because the next joint is the same age. Galvanized pipe can also collect lead from an upstream lead service line, so it is worth replacing both.

Repiping versus water line replacement

Two different pipes, often confused. The buried service line carries water from the curb stop to the house and is covered on our water line replacement page. Repiping replaces everything inside, starting at the main shutoff.

If your service line is lead or galvanized and your interior pipes are galvanized too, doing both together means one water outage and one permit window, and it removes the old metal from end to end.

PEX or copper for the new pipes

MaterialAdvantagesConsiderations
PEXFlexible, fewer fittings, fishes through walls with fewer openingsMust be kept out of direct sunlight and away from high heat sources.
CopperLong track record, rigid and durableMore openings needed in plaster walls, and higher material cost.

In plaster-walled historic homes, PEX usually means fewer and smaller wall openings, which keeps restoration costs down.

How a repipe is done while you live there

  • A plan maps each fixture and the route for new lines, favoring closets and basement ceilings.
  • New lines are run while the old ones stay in service.
  • Fixtures are switched to the new pipes a section at a time, keeping water outages short.
  • The system is pressure-tested and inspected under a city permit.
  • Wall openings are patched, and old pipe is either removed or abandoned in place.

Lead solder and older copper

Copper supply pipe installed before the late 1980s was often joined with solder that contained lead. Those joints are a smaller concern than a lead service line, but they are one more reason owners of older homes choose to repipe. If a water test at the tap shows lead and your service line is not lead, the interior plumbing is the next place to look.

Repiping in Hamilton and nearby

We repipe homes in ZIP codes 45011, 45013 and 45015, most often older houses with original galvanized lines. Rental owners in Oxford repipe between tenants for the same reasons. If you are dealing with one active leak before planning a repipe, start with burst pipe repair.

What repiping costs depends on

A repipe is priced on fixtures and access more than square footage. These factors matter most.

  • Number of fixtures and bathrooms to reconnect.
  • Material choice: PEX or copper.
  • Wall type, since plaster takes more care than drywall.
  • Access from an unfinished basement or attic.
  • Permit and inspection, required for a repipe.

Insurance does not pay to replace aging pipes. It may cover sudden damage from a leak, so do not wait for the next failure to plan the repipe.
