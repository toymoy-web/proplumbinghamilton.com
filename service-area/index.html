Hamilton, Ohio, not Hamilton County

The name causes confusion often enough that it is worth saying clearly. We work in the City of Hamilton, the county seat of Butler County, and in the Butler County communities around it. Hamilton County is a different county to the south that contains Cincinnati, with its own sewer district and plumbing permit office. We are also not connected with Hamilton, Ontario, in Canada.

If your address says Hamilton, OH with a 45011, 45013 or 45015 ZIP code, you are in the right place. Addresses in Fairfield Township, Liberty Township and parts of West Chester also use Hamilton or nearby ZIP codes, and we cover those too. When in doubt, give us the street address when you call and we will confirm coverage on the spot rather than guess from the ZIP alone.

Hamilton, the center of our coverage

Inside the City of Hamilton we cover ZIP codes 45011, 45013 and 45015, from German Village, Dayton Lane and Rossville to the neighborhoods out along Route 4 and Route 129. The city runs its own water, gas, electric and wastewater utilities, and owners are responsible for the full sewer lateral under city code section 931.031.

Older houses here are the most likely to have galvanized supply pipe, clay sewer laterals and lead service lines, which is why water line replacement and sewer line repair are common Hamilton calls.

Which utility to call, and when to call a plumber

Water, sewer and gas utilities own their mains and meters. Everything on your side of the meter or curb stop is private plumbing. In Hamilton that line is set by city code for sewer laterals and by the curb stop for water. Fairfield, Oxford and Trenton each run their own water systems with their own rules, and homes on private wells have no water utility at all.

A simple rule: if several neighbors lose water or back up at the same time, call the utility first. If it is only your house, call a plumber. Utilities repair their own mains at no charge to you, so ruling that out first can save the cost of a service call.

  • Sewage backing up only in your house: a plumber, usually your lateral.
  • Water main break in the street: the water utility.
  • Smell of gas anywhere: leave, then call the gas utility or 911 from outside.
  • Meter spinning with everything off: a plumber to find the leak on your side.

What happens when you call from outside the city

The call works the same way whether you are in German Village or on a rural road in Ross Township. You describe the problem, you are told whether it needs someone tonight, and you get a realistic arrival window. Drive time from Hamilton to the edges of the area is longer, and you will hear that honestly rather than a promise that cannot be kept.

Permits follow the address. Hamilton issues its own plumbing permits under a city Master Registration. Across the rest of Butler County, outside Hamilton and Middletown, plumbing permits come from the Butler County General Health District. Both inspect to the Ohio Plumbing Code, which is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), so the standard is the same even where the paperwork differs.
