Where Lindenwald is

WVXU's 2022 profile describes Lindenwald as an old railroad town bordered by the train tracks and Ohio 4 on the east and the Great Miami River on the west. It stretches from the border with the City of Fairfield north to Knightsbridge Drive.

Inside that area, the city's street map shows Pleasant Avenue, Dixie Highway, Benninghofen Avenue, Van Hook Avenue, Madison Avenue and Zimmerman Avenue as the main through streets. Many east-west streets carry the names of 19th century Ohio governors, such as Tiffin, Corwin, Bishop and Hayes. Nearly every street address here uses ZIP 45015.

Homes in Lindenwald

The community was called the Village of Lindenwald by the 1850s, though it was never incorporated, and it was annexed into Hamilton in 1908. WVXU notes housing that ranges from stately homes along Pleasant Avenue to American four-squares, Craftsman bungalows and a few tiny homes.

City data puts about 61 percent of homes as owner-occupied and 39 percent as rentals. That mix matters for plumbing calls, because a tenant usually needs the landlord's approval before a repair goes ahead.

With more than 4,000 households, Lindenwald produces every kind of plumbing call. The most useful starting points are below, each with its own detailed page.

  • Drain cleaning for a slow kitchen sink, a backed-up floor drain or a main line that keeps clogging.

  • Sewer line repair when a camera shows a break between the house and the street.
  • Sump pump service for basements that rely on a pump during heavy rain.

Owners and renters: who calls, and when

If you rent, report the problem to your landlord or property manager first, and shut off the fixture valve if water is spreading. Many landlords are happy for the tenant to call once they have approved the repair.

If you own a Lindenwald rental, give tenants a phone number for after-hours problems and show them the main shutoff. A tenant who can close the main in the first few minutes saves you the cost of drying out a basement.

City rules that apply in Lindenwald

Lindenwald is inside the city, so homes get City of Hamilton water from the city's wells and Hamilton's own gas utility. Hamilton code section 931.031 makes the owner responsible for the sewer lateral from the house to the public sewer. New plumbing work follows the 2024 Ohio Plumbing Code, which is based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC).

Plumbing permits here come from Hamilton's building department, not the county. The neighborhood also has more parks than any other in the city, from Benninghofen Park to the 313-acre Joyce Park off River Road. If a Fairfield address is just across the border, its water comes from Fairfield's own utility instead.
