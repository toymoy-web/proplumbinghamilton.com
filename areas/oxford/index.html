Oxford's hard water comes first

Oxford's water is among the hardest in the region. City reports put it at about 350 to 380 mg/L, compared with roughly 140 mg/L for Hamilton's treated water. Hard water leaves scale in water heaters, faucet cartridges, shower heads and dishwashers, and it shortens their life.

The city is working on it. In June 2026, Oxford City Council approved a $24.8 million contract for a new water treatment facility that adds membrane softening. Until it is running, most homes still get unsoftened water, so a whole-house water softener and regular water heater flushing remain the practical defenses.

Once the new plant is softening water, a home softener may become unnecessary. If yours is due for replacement, it is worth asking whether a repair can carry it until then.

Leak in an Oxford rental or home? What to do before we get there

Find the shutoff with the tenant

Student houses often have the main valve in a basement corner the tenants have never seen. Talk them through it on the phone if you manage the property remotely.

Switch off wet circuits

Older Oxford houses carry wiring near basement plumbing. If water reaches outlets or a panel, stay out and shut power off from a dry spot.

Move belongings off the floor

Tenants should lift laptops, bags and bedding clear of the water and take photos for the owner and any renters insurance claim.

Rentals near campus and what breaks in them

Houses with five or six tenants run far more water through a single system than a family home does. Toilets run constantly, shower valves wear out early, kitchen drains fill with grease, and water heaters sized for a family cannot keep up.

Our toilet repair and drain cleaning pages cover the two most common rental calls. Landlords often schedule fixes between semesters, when a house is empty and repairs do not disrupt tenants.

  • Tell tenants where the main shutoff is on move-in day.
  • Check under sinks and around toilets during every turnover.
  • Flush water heaters yearly, and more often on Oxford's hard water.
  • Replace rubber washer hoses with braided lines.

What to do when a tenant reports a leak

Most rental leaks start small. How fast someone acts in the first few minutes decides whether it stays small.

  • Have the tenant close the valve under the fixture, or the main shutoff if the leak is in a wall or ceiling.
  • Ask for photos of the leak and anything wet before cleanup starts.
  • If water reaches light fixtures or outlets, the tenant should switch off that circuit at the panel.
  • Call us with the address and the tenant's contact so access can be arranged directly.

Rental toilets are a frequent source of silent leaks. The Ohio Plumbing Code, based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), caps new toilets at 1.6 gallons per flush, so replacing an old high-flow toilet during a turnover also lowers the water bill.

Owner-occupied homes outside the campus area
