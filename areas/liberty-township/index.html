A township built in two decades

Liberty Township grew from 9,249 residents in 1990 to 22,819 in 2000 and about 44,000 today. Most of that growth came as subdivisions, with Cincinnati Children's Liberty Campus opening in 2008, the Liberty Way interchange in 2009 and Liberty Center in 2015.

Homes built that quickly share equipment of similar age. Whole streets of water heaters, sump pumps and fixtures installed within a few years of each other are now wearing out at about the same time. If a neighbor's water heater just failed, yours may not be far behind.

Water heater or sump failure in Liberty Township? Before we arrive

Stop the water source

For a leaking water heater, close the cold valve on top of the tank. For a supply leak, close the main, usually on the basement wall facing the street.

Kill power to the basement circuits

Finished lower levels often have outlets near the floor. If water is spreading, shut those breakers off before walking through it.

Save what you can

Lift rugs, furniture legs and stored boxes. If the sump pit is overflowing, check that the pump is plugged in and the float is free.

Finished basements raise the stakes

Many Liberty homes have finished basements with drywall, carpet and living space. A failed sump pump or a leaking water heater there damages far more than it would in an unfinished basement. A battery backup on the sump, a water alarm near the heater, and braided washer hoses are small costs that protect a large investment.

  • Test the sump pump each spring by filling the pit with water.
  • Put a water alarm on the floor beside the water heater.
  • Check the water heater label for its age.
  • Know where the main shutoff is before you need it.

Why early PEX fittings leak, and what to check

Many homes built here in the late 1990s and 2000s were plumbed with PEX, which was newer then. The pipe itself holds up well. Leaks tend to show at fittings: a crimp ring that was not fully compressed, a brass fitting affected by aggressive water, or a connection stressed where the pipe was pulled too tight around a corner.

Check the exposed PEX in the basement ceiling near the water heater and under sinks for green or white crust on brass fittings, which signals a slow weep.

A small weep at one fitting is a simple repair. Crust on many fittings is a sign to have the whole system looked at. The Ohio Plumbing Code, based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), also requires a drain pan under a water heater where a leak would cause damage, a worthwhile addition in any finished basement.
