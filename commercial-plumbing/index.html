Grease, restaurants and kitchen lines

Food businesses produce grease that hardens in lines and interceptors. The Ohio Plumbing Code, based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), requires grease interceptors for commercial kitchens. Owners must have them pumped and maintained on a regular schedule.

When a kitchen line slows anyway, hydro jetting clears grease from the pipe wall far better than a cable. Keeping the interceptor maintained is what keeps it from coming back quickly.

Keeping a business open during repairs

The first question on any commercial call is what has to stay working. A restaurant may need one restroom and the kitchen sinks kept live while another line is repaired. An office may only be able to lose water before 7 a.m. Planning around that is part of the job, not an extra.

Isolation valves for each floor or unit often let a crew work without shutting off the whole property. Where it does not, adding them during the first repair pays off on every future call. Older downtown buildings in Hamilton frequently lack them.

What commercial plumbing costs depends on

Commercial jobs vary more than residential ones. These factors set the price.

  • Scope: one repair versus a maintenance program.
  • After-hours scheduling to avoid closing.
  • Equipment size, such as commercial water heaters and larger drains.
  • Access in occupied spaces.
  • Permits and inspections for permitted work.

Commercial property policies vary widely. Keep repair records, since they help if a claim is ever filed after a loss.
