Toilet Repair & Installation in Hamilton, OH

Toilet repair in Hamilton, OH covers every common toilet problem. That includes a toilet that runs all day, one that rocks or leaks at the base, a weak or double flush, and full replacement. Most repairs are small parts inside the tank or the seal underneath.

What your toilet is telling you

  • Refills on its own every few minutes: flapper leak.
  • Water on the floor after flushing: wax ring or supply line.
  • Rocks when you sit: loose bolts or damaged flange.
  • Gurgles when other drains run: a vent or main line issue.

Why a small leak at the base matters

The wax ring seals the toilet to the drain flange. When it fails, water seeps under the toilet on each flush, often without showing much on the surface. In older Hamilton homes with wood subfloors over basements, that slow leak can soften the floor and stain the ceiling below.

If the toilet rocks, the seal cannot stay tight. Resetting it involves new bolts, a new ring, and sometimes a flange repair. Check the basement ceiling under the bathroom for stains, since that is often the first sign.

Test for a silent leak in two minutes

  • Add some food dye to the tank water, leaving the bowl alone.
  • Do not flush for 15 minutes.
  • Dye showing up in the bowl means water is slipping past the flapper.
  • That slow slip is behind many unexplained jumps in the water bill.

If the bill is still high after fixing the toilet, our leak detection service can find what else is leaking.

Replacing a toilet

A new toilet makes sense when the bowl is cracked, the toilet clogs constantly, or it uses several gallons per flush. Modern models use far less water, and the Ohio Plumbing Code, based on the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), caps new toilets at 1.6 gallons per flush. Older bathrooms sometimes have an unusual rough-in distance from the wall, so that gets measured before choosing a new one.

If a toilet clogs every week and others in the house are slow too, the problem may be in the main line, covered on our drain cleaning page.

Supply lines and shutoff valves

The small supply line between the wall valve and the tank is a common leak point. Older solid lines crack and plastic nuts fail. A braided stainless line costs little and is worth replacing whenever the toilet is worked on. If the shutoff valve will not close fully, it should be replaced too, since it is the only quick way to stop an overflow.

What toilet repair costs depends on

Most toilet repairs are quick. These factors raise or lower the price.

  • The part: flapper, fill valve, gasket or wax ring.
  • Pulling the toilet for seal or flange work.
  • Flange or subfloor damage found underneath.
  • New toilet supply and installation.
  • Timing of the visit.

Toilet parts are maintenance. Damage from a sudden overflow may be covered, but slow leaks under the toilet usually are not.
